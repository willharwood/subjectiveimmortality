What this book is about, why I chose to write it (and how), what it tries to achieve. Anti-AI pledge.


Introduction

Immortalities: the cosmic joke of objective immortality, with examples. Subjective experience, and the difficulty of imagining oblivion. Our physicalist view. Where we want to get to: the great either/or.


Ways to be immortal
      Three quotations to set the scene

Lucretius: using atomism to dispell the fear of the gods. An argument to not fear future oblivion because of past oblivion, which we turn into one for no oblivion, past or future, a direction Montaigne noticed in Lucretius. From a physical soul to Hume's bundled consciousness. Using the common-sense view to vanquish oblivion.


      Dualism, materialism, and idealism

Three conceptions of the universe: dualism (Descartes), materialism (Epicurus), idealism (Berkeley). Show how dualism necessarily collapses into either materialism or idealism -- and plump for materialism.


      Materialism and determinism

Do we require deterministic materialism? Use Laplace's Demon. Free will as a Swerve in nature? Too cute. Conclude that it does not matter either way.


      Boltzmann and Nietzsche

The Eternal Returns of Nietzsche and Boltzmann. Boltzmann brains haunting any theory of the far future, a very unsatisfactory version of subjective immortality. The crux of the matter: subjective continuity.


      Functionalism (are we nothing but the pattern?)

Functionalism as an attempt at subjective continuity: if you are a pattern then if that pattern returns, so do you. An unsatisfying conclusion.


      Quantum suicide

Another approach to subjective immortality, and why it doesn't work.


      An inkling of the problem of personal immortality

Returning to the subjective continuity question of Boltzmann and Nietzsche.


      The three Ships of Theseus

Pushing at intuition of what constitutes an individual and personal continuity. Using the Ship of Theseus as a metaphor, and physicalism as the principle, to argue that the history of an individual should not matter. The original ship, Hobbes' version, and a third version. The question of identity is one thing, sure this one can be the "real" ship. We are asking something different that takes place in the now.


      The moral consequences of materialism

Clarify the type of materialism we mean through an argument from morality against it.


      Parfit's Non-Identity problem, his Repugnant Conclusion, and the Lonely Conclusion

From Parfit to the Lonely Conclusion: that a world in which there is a single supremely happy individual actually might be the best world. Teases at the non-problem of not being born.


Consciousness

Not really a survey of current theories of consciousness. IIT, global workspace, etc. The hard problem, and the many ways it is avoided in literature. Why materialism does not remove free will (for a certain value of free will). Panpsychism and its deficiencies. The impossibility of philosophical zombies as a way to underline what materialism really means to us.


      It is not what you think

The strangeness of consciousness.


      Three mysteries

The mysteries of life, intelligence, and consciousness, and why consciousness is the difficult one.


      Some assumptions on the nature of subjective experience
      Panpsychism

A class of theories of consciousness. There can be "something like" to be an electron at the same time as not needing to assume that electrons differ in ways we physically cannot detect (if this were not so, our arguments would fall apart).
